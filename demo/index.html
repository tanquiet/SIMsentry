SIM-swap fraud detection & investigation

Catch the SIM swap before the one-time password is used.

SwapSentry scores every SIM-swap event with three independent models, lists the evidence behind the score, and has a small language model draft an investigation report. Every report is fact-checked before it is shown. A human analyst makes the final decision.

The problem

A SIM swap hands your phone number, and every OTP, to someone else.

The attacker convinces the carrier to move the victim's number onto a SIM they control. From then on, the bank's one-time passwords go to the attacker.

Gather details

The attacker phishes or buys the victim's personal details.

Swap the SIM

They pose as the victim and get the number moved to a new SIM or eSIM.

Intercept OTPs

The victim loses signal. Login and payment codes now reach the attacker.

SwapSentry steps in

The swap event is scored in milliseconds and risky ones are held for review.

What gives a fraudulent swap away

SwapSentry reads 11 signals from each event. You can type them in plain English, upload a CSV or JSON file, or fill in a form.

Why not just one model?

LightGBM + Isolation Forest are very precise on numbers, and the Isolation Forest also catches patterns that look nothing like normal traffic. They only see numbers.

Laya (ModernBERT-large, 421M parameters, fine-tuned) reads the event as text. It gives a second opinion and decides when a human must look.

Qwen 3.5 2B takes 12 to 40 seconds per report, so it runs only on flagged cases. It explains the decision but never makes it.

Evidence rules are plain if-then checks. They are the only source of the evidence list, so nothing in it is made up.

    Honest limits
    
  
    What this prototype is not.
    
  
    
    
    Scope
      
      
    • Synthetic data only. It has not been validated on real subscriber traffic.
      • 
      
    • Decision support, not a verdict. A human reviews every flag. An actual "hold" would need telecom integration that this prototype does not have.
      • 
      
    • No authentication. It is meant to run on 127.0.0.1 only.
      • 
      
    • Not DPDP-ready. There is no retention, deletion or grievance workflow yet.
      • 
    
    
    
    Known weak spots
      
      
    • Laya is about 83% accurate on deliberately hard boundary cases, and 8% of those are still confidently wrong.
      • 
      
    • Borderline scores (p ≈ 0.4–0.6) can change slightly with batch size. The human-review band covers these cases.
      • 
      
    • Qwen sometimes invents numbers. The fact-check catches this (see the "Models disagree" replay), and the system then falls back to a template report.
      • 
      
    • The text reader does not guess vague wording such as "recently" or "different city". It reports those values as missing.
      •
